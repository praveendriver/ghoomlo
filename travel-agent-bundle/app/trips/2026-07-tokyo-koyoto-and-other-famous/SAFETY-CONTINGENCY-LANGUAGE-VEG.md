## If you get separated

### Pre-departure prep
1. **Live location sharing:** Set up continuous Google Maps or WhatsApp location sharing between all family members for the entire trip
2. **ID cards for kids:** Create laminated cards for each child with:
   - Parents' phone numbers (India +91 and local Japan SIM if you get one)
   - Hotel names and addresses in English & Japanese
   - "I am lost. Please help me find my parents." in Japanese (below)
   - A recent photo of the family

### Agreed protocol at EVERY venue
**Before entering ANY crowded place (USJ, DisneySea, stations, temples, shopping streets):**
- Point out a specific meeting point: "If we get separated, wait here at this fountain/statue/shop entrance"
- Show kids staff uniforms (park staff, station staff) and explain they wear name badges
- Take a photo of each child at the start of the day showing what they're wearing

### What to teach kids
- **Stay where you are** if you realize you're lost — don't wander looking for parents
- Approach someone in uniform: station staff, park staff, store employees, police
- Show the ID card
- If at a train station, look for the "Station Office" (駅事務室) — usually near ticket gates
- If in a park (USJ/DisneySea), find the nearest staff member in costume or uniform

### Koban (police boxes)
**What they are:** Small neighborhood police stations (交番 / kōban), recognizable by a red light or police symbol outside. Found near major stations, tourist areas, and throughout neighborhoods.

**Services for tourists:**
- Report lost children or items
- Get directions (many have maps)
- English may be limited, but they will help — use translation apps or show written Japanese

**Lost child procedure:** Police will keep the child safe, contact you via hotel or your phone, and may take them to a larger police station if needed. Always call your hotel immediately if separated — hotels can coordinate with police.

## Missed train / transport failure

### If you miss a Shinkansen
- Non-reserved seats: just board the next train on the same route (seats not guaranteed)
- Reserved seats: you forfeit the reservation, but the base ticket is still valid — board the next train in non-reserved cars, or pay to re-reserve
- See your rail-specific section for JR Pass rules

### Backup: Taxis
- **Intercity taxi is NOT practical** — Tokyo ↔ Kyoto would cost ¥100,000+ and take 6-7 hours; always take the next train instead
- **Local taxi for short distances:** Available at every station taxi rank
- **GO taxi app:** Japan's main taxi app
  - ⚠ COULD NOT VERIFY current English support or international card acceptance in 2026
  - Stations always have taxi ranks if the app doesn't work
  - Rough local taxi cost: ¥700-1000 base fare + ~¥400/km; a 5km ride ≈ ¥2500-3000

### Late-night fallback
- Last trains typically 23:30-00:30; check BEFORE your evening activity
- If stranded: take a taxi, or wait at a 24h konbini / Dennys / manga cafe until first trains (~5am)
- Your hotels (Westin Miyako, JW Marriott) have 24h front desks — call them if stuck

## Money & ATMs

Japan is still **cash-heavy** outside major chains. Always carry ¥10,000-20,000 cash per day for meals, temple entry fees, small shops, taxis.

### Which ATMs accept foreign cards (VERIFIED)
1. **Seven Bank (7-Eleven)** — most reliable
   - 28,000+ ATMs across Japan, 24/7 at most 7-Eleven stores
   - Accepts: Visa, Mastercard, Maestro, Cirrus, PLUS, Amex, JCB, Discover, Diners, UnionPay
   - https://www.sevenbank.co.jp/intlcard/ (verified)
   - ⚠ Note: "Cards with the same logo may still not work in some cases" — always have backup

2. **Japan Post Bank ATMs**
   - At post offices and FamilyMart konbini
   - Accepts: Visa, Mastercard, Maestro, Cirrus, PLUS, JCB, UnionPay, Discover
   - Hours: 7am-11pm at post offices; ~0:05am-11:55pm at FamilyMart
   - Withdrawal limit: ¥50,000 per transaction
   - Fee: ¥220 per withdrawal (plus your bank's fees)
   - Balance inquiry NOT available
   - https://www.jp-bank.japanpost.jp/en/ias/en_ias_index.html (verified)

### Before you leave India
- **Notify your bank** you'll be using cards in Japan (dates July 7-18)

- Note your bank's 24h helpline number
- Consider a forex card as backup (check which ATM networks it supports)

## Eating vegetarian in Japan

This is the **big challenge**. Japanese cuisine uses hidden fish-based ingredients in almost everything, even "vegetable" dishes.

### The hidden fish-stock problem
- **Dashi (出汁):** Fish stock made from bonito flakes (katsuobushi), dried kelp (kombu), or sardines. Used in:
  - Miso soup
  - Ramen broth (even "vegetable" ramen)
  - Tempura dipping sauce
  - Takoyaki, okonomiyaki batter
  - Sauces, marinades, "vegetable" stir-fries
- **Bonito flakes (katsuobushi / 鰹節):** Shaved dried fish, sprinkled on okonomiyaki, takoyaki, salads
- **Oyster sauce, fish sauce:** Common in Chinese-style dishes

**Strict vegetarians cannot eat most Japanese restaurant food without explicitly asking.**

### Vegetarian card (printable)
⚠ COULD NOT VERIFY a single authoritative template, but you can create one with:

```

Thank you for your help.
```

Laminate this and show it at every restaurant.

### Safe vegetarian options

#### Indian restaurants (EASIEST)
- Plentiful in Tokyo and Kyoto
- Staff speak English/Hindi
- Search Google Maps: "Indian restaurant near me" in Higashiyama (Kyoto) or Roppongi (Tokyo)
- Chains: Namaste, Ganga, Samrat, Sagar, countless others

#### Shojin ryori (精進料理)
- Traditional Buddhist temple vegan cuisine
- No meat, fish, or pungent vegetables (onion/garlic)
- Higher-end, expensive (¥3000-8000/person)
- Kyoto temples sometimes offer shojin ryori meals — ask your hotel concierge
- ⚠ COULD NOT VERIFY specific restaurant names near Westin Miyako, but Google "shojin ryori Kyoto"

#### Vegan/vegetarian restaurant chains
⚠ COULD NOT VERIFY current locations/hours for 2026, but these chains exist:
- **Ain Soph** — vegan burgers, pancakes, desserts (Tokyo locations)
- **T's Tantan** — vegan ramen (Tokyo Station, others)
- **Vegan chains** — search "vegan restaurant Tokyo" / "vegan restaurant Kyoto" on Google Maps day-of

#### CoCo Ichibanya curry (verified chain)
- Nationwide curry chain
- Offers "vegetable curry" (野菜カレー / yasai curry)
- ⚠ **ASK about dashi in the roux** — some curry bases may contain fish stock
- Allergennfo PDF on their site: https://www.ichibanya.co.jp/menu/detail.html?id=265 (verified)

#### Konbini safe items
- **Onigiri (rice balls):** Most contain fish/meat. Safe-ish options: 梅 (umeboshi / pickled plum), 昆布 (konbu / kelp — but check for bonito), おにぎり野菜 (vegetable)
  - ⚠ Read labels carefully; "vegetable" onigiri may still have dashi
- **Salads** — check dressing ingredients
- **Inarizushi** (sweet tofu pockets with rice) — usually safe, but ask
- **Fruit, yogurt, bread, pastries** — safest konbini options

#### What to AVOID
- Ramen (broth is always dashi-based unless explicitly vegan)
- Udon/soba (dashi broth)
- Okonomiyaki, takoyaki (dashi + bonito flakes)
- Tempura (dipping sauce has dashi)
- Sushi (obvious)
- "Vegetable" anything at non-vegetarian restaurants without asking

## July weather & typhoons

### Typical July weather
- **Temperature:** 28-35°C (hot and humid)
- **Rainy season (tsuyu):** Usually ends early-to-mid July, but can linger — expect occasional rain
- **Typhoon season BEGINS in July** — peak is August-September, but typhoons can hit in July

### Heat precautions for kids
- **Hydration:** Carry water bottles, refill at hotel/konbini. Kids should drink every 30-60 min
- **Breaks:** Schedule indoor (air-conditioned) breaks every 2-3 hours — museums, malls, cafes
- **Hats, sunscreen, lightweight clothing**
- Watch for heat exhaustion: dizziness, nausea, extreme fatigue → get to shade, cool down, hydrate, seek medical help if severe

### If a typhoon hits
- **Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA):** https://www.jma.go.jp/jma/en/menu.html (verified)
  - Typhoon warnings, weather advisories
  - Available in English
- **JNTO Safety Tips app:** Download before you go — push notifications for earthquakes, typhoons, tsunami
  - https://www.jnto.go.jp/safety-tips/eng/ (verified)
- **What happens:**
  - Trains, buses, flights WILL STOP if typhoon warning is issued
  - Stay at your hotel until "all clear"
  - Hotels are built to withstand typhoons — you are safe inside
  - Avoid going out during warnings
  - Check JR East/West websites for train status
  - Your hotels will provide updates
