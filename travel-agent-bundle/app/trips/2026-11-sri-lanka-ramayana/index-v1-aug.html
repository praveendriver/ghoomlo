The four things to buy

Ceylon's signature purchases

You asked for gemstones and unique shopping. Sri Lanka's four authentic categories — each with a "buy it right" note. Gem and jewellery spend is treated as shopping, not travel budget, so it sits outside the ₹7L trip cap. The one rule that protects you on stones: buy only with an NGJA (National Gem & Jewellery Authority) certificate.

In Sri Lanka the easy, cheap, safe-with-kids move is to hire one car with an English-speaking driver-guide for the entire trip. Hill-country roads are winding — a local driver is worth it. In Colombo, PickMe (the local ride app) handles short hops.

Before you fly — ETAs & docs
T-2 weeks

Sri Lanka requires an ETA (Electronic Travel Authorization) per traveller. Apply online for all four — the three Indian passports and the one US passport go in as separate applications. Verify the current fee (it has changed a few times) before paying.

Per-traveller (differs by passport)
  • 3 × Indian passport → ETA each
  • 1 × US passport (the child) → ETA separately
  • 6-month passport validity for all four
  • Return ticket + hotel proof handy
Money & connectivity
  • Some USD cash + a card; LKR from ATMs on arrival
  • Local eSIM (Dialog/Mobitel) at the airport
  • Card that works for gem purchases (or bank transfer)

Throughout
Food (the veg risk)
  • Say "no fish, no Maldive fish, no egg" every order

  • Sealed water only; carry kid ORS

Temple etiquette
  • Shoulders + knees covered; shoes off at temples

  • Hold the kids close in temple crowds
  • 112 = Sri Lanka emergency number
Carrying gemstones home
Tue departure + BLR arrival

Keep every stone's NGJA certificate + purchase invoice with you, not in checked bags. Carry stones in your hand luggage. On arrival in India, declare them honestly at customs — certified stones with invoices are straightforward; undeclared high-value goods are the risk.
