  5. Light modest layers — a scarf/stole + something covering shoulders & knees for temples and churches.
  6. Set a Saturday alarm — Day 2 wants an early, cool start at Old Goa.

🛂 Documents — the per-person bit that matters

  • Domestic flight (BLR→GOI): carry a government photo ID for each adult. For the kids, carry ID too.
  • ⚠️ Your US-citizen child travels on a US passport. For domestic India travel that's fine as photo ID, but carry it (and ideally the child's OCI card if they hold one). It becomes relevant at two Ponda temples that reportedly bar foreigner entry — see Day 3. Decide in advance how you'll answer if asked.
  • No visa/immigration anything — this is a domestic trip.

📱 Apps to install before you fly

  • Google Maps (download the Goa offline area).
  • GoaMiles — Goa's official app-taxi service (useful backup to the hotel car). ⚠️ Availability in deep-south/rural spots is patchy; the hotel car is the reliable option for day trips.
  • A UPI app (GPay/PhonePe) — widely accepted even at small places, but still carry cash for temples/jeeps.
  • Your Bonvoy app — for mobile check-in and to show Titanium status.

  • Temples/churches: covered shoulders & knees; slip-off shoes (you'll remove them at temples). A stole for everyone.
  • Dudhsagar (Day 4): non-slip sandals/shoes that can get wet, a full change of clothes per kid, towel, water, dry snacks, a zip-lock for phones (there's a stream crossing).
  • Everywhere: hats, high-SPF sunscreen, mosquito repellent, refillable water.

PART 3 — EATING VEGETARIAN (the real challenge in Goa)

The hidden-fish problem

Goan cuisine is built on fish, prawn and pork. Even "vegetable" dishes can hide fish stock, dried fish, or shrimp (balchão) paste, and beach-shack kitchens share woks. Baked goods and some breads can contain egg. So: always show the veg card, and always say "no fish stock, no egg."

Show this at every restaurant (also on the Cheat Card)

We are PURE VEGETARIAN. No meat, no fish, no seafood/prawn, NO EGG.
No fish stock or shrimp paste in the gravy, please.
Milk, cheese, paneer, butter are OK. Is this dish vegetarian?

Where you'll actually eat well (verified anchors)

  • PURE VEGNavtara — 100% vegetarian chain; outlets in Margao, Panjim (Municipal Market) and Ponda (opp. Municipal Garden). South Indian / North Indian / Chinese. ✅ VERIFIED (navtara.in). Your reliable fallback in every area. 📍 Maps: "Navtara Veg Restaurant" + the town
  • PURE VEGThe Rasoda, Old Goa — pure-veg, North Indian/Rajasthani, right by the churches. ✅ VERIFIED (therasoda.com). ⚠️ no hours online → call +91 84477 43753. 📍 Shop 20/21 Milroc Kadamba, off NH-748
  • BACKUPUdupi Pure Veg, Ella — South Indian, ~1.3 km from Bom Jesus. ⚠️ listing only.
  • PANJIMCafé Tato / Café Bhonsle — old-school Goan vegetarian cafés (bhaji-puri, buns, samosas). ⚠️ listing/snippet; Café Tato near the Municipal Garden.
  • NEAR HOTELThe Veggies Pure Veg, Utorda — pure veg, next to the hotel beach. ⚠️ listing (small review count).

Goan VEG dishes worth seeking (and the look-alikes)

  • VEGKhatkhate — mixed vegetables with toor & chana dal. Usually safe.
  • VEGVegetable caldin — veg in coconut milk — only order when the menu literally says "vegetable" (the classic caldine is fish).
  • VEGVegetable xacuti — the veg version of the famous xacuti (originally meat).
  • VEGAlsande tondak — a cowpea (black-eyed-bean) dish.

⛔ Traps & hazards

  • ⛔ "Shantadurga Bhojanalay" next to Mangueshi temple serves a fish plate — it is NOT the temple canteen. Don't be fooled by the holy-sounding name. (Day 3 trap.)
  • Spice-farm buffets (Tropical, Sahakari) put fish/chicken on the same line → we're skipping those buffets (plan is Navtara + plantation walk only).
  • Beach shacks are seafood-first with shared kitchens — treat as snack/drinks only unless they clearly cook veg separately.
  • Egg hides in cakes, some breads and Portuguese pastries — always ask.

🏨 At ITC Grand Goa

Six venues (✅ Marriott site): Pavilion (24h, all-day), Kebabs & Kurries (Indian, 12:30–3 & 7–11), Ottimo (Italian, 7–11), Tempero (⚠️ Goan/Portuguese — seafood-heavy), Farol bar, Pool Bar. PART 6 — 🧠 HARD-WON GOTCHAS

⛩️ Temples

  • Dress code: covered shoulders & knees; shoes off before entering. Keep a stole handy.
  • Midday break: many Goan temples close for a few midday hours — go before ~12:30 or confirm the day's timing.
  • ⚠️ Foreigner-entry rule at Shantadurga (Kavlem) & Mahalasa (Mardol) — see Day 3; we anchor on Mangueshi (no such rule) and call ahead for the others.
  • ⛔ Don't eat at "Shantadurga Bhojanalay" by Mangueshi — it serves fish.

⛪ Old Goa churches

  • Modest clothing; it's an active place of worship. Visit around Mass times at the Basilica (ask concierge).
  • Lots of open, shadeless walking and little food at the site — carry water; eat right after at The Rasoda.
  • Keep valuables close; it's a busy tourist cluster.

💦 Dudhsagar

  • Jeep only. ⛔ Walking the railway track to the falls is banned and fined by the Forest Dept; getting on/off at the Dudhsagar railway stop is an offence. Don't improvise.
  • The jeep track is bumpy; there's a short walk + stream crossing at the end — hence the change of clothes.
  • Food is sparse out there — carry a hotel-packed veg lunch/snacks.

🚕 Cars & cash

  • Goa taxis are unmetered — agree the fare/route first; the hotel car avoids the haggling.
  • Cash-first at temples, church tickets, the jeep, and small veg eateries.

🏖️ Beaches & shacks

  • Shacks are seafood-first with shared kitchens — snacks/drinks only unless veg is cooked separately.
  • Swim only on flagged, lifeguarded stretches.
