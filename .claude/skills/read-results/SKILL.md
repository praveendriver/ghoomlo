# Read results

A handful of responses can be read to mean anything. The discipline here is to compare against what was decided beforehand, separate what people did from what they said, and say plainly how much weight the result can bear.

## Steps

1. **Find the plan.** Open the study plan in `research/` and its thresholds. If no thresholds were written down, say so first, and treat the reading as exploratory.
2. **Check who answered.** How many, how recruited, which cities, how many were screened out. Friends of the founder answer kindly; note it.
3. **Read the open answers before the numbers.** Group them by what people describe doing, in their words. Quote two or three that are typical and one that is surprising. Do not quote anything that identifies a person.
4. **Then the numbers,** as counts ("11 of 24"). Compare each to its threshold: met, missed, or too close to call.
5. **Look for disagreement.** Where a ranking says one thing and the described behaviour says another, believe the behaviour and point out the gap.
6. **State the finding and its strength** using this scale: *observed behaviour* > *reported past behaviour* > *stated preference* > *our belief*.
7. **Recommend a decision** tied to the thresholds: proceed, test further (name the next test), or drop.

## What to produce

In chat, short enough to read on a phone:

- **Headline** — one sentence on what the study says.
- **What we learned** — three to five points, strongest evidence first.
- **What we still do not know.**
- **Recommendation** — and what would change it.

Then update the project's memory so the next session starts from it:

- Add a dated entry to `DISCOVERY_NOTES.md` in the format already used there (source, evidence strength, what was found, cautions).
- Update the status of affected ideas in `research/idea-log.md`.
- If the result changes what Ghoomlo is for, say so and ask before editing `PRODUCT_BRIEF.md`; that is the founder's call.

## Cautions worth repeating in every reading

- Tens of responses show direction. They do not show how common something is across India.
- People who rank an idea first have not shown they would use it; look for a stronger signal before building.
- A study answers the question it asked. If the ideas have moved on since it was written, say which current ideas it does and does not speak to.
