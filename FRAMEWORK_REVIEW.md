# Product and delivery framework review

**Reviewed:** 5 November 2026  
**Documents:** [Product brief](PRODUCT_BRIEF.md), [staged launch plan](LAUNCH_PLAN.md)  
**Scope:** Product discovery, user needs, UX, accessibility, privacy, engineering readiness, reliability, and launch gates.

## Assessment

The documents are useful starting hypotheses and a credible staged path. They are not evidence that the market, segment, feature scope, or business model is validated. I strengthened the discovery loop, added India-wide cohort coverage, sharpened user outcome and usability measures, and made security, accessibility, privacy, and operational release gates more explicit.

| Area | Assessment | Changes made / remaining work |
|---|---|---|
| Product discovery | Good sequence, but the original brief risked treating a chosen segment and feature list as established facts. | Marked the segment and problem as hypotheses; added outcome-to-opportunity mapping, observation, assumption tests, and ongoing research. Still needs actual user evidence. |
| India-wide user needs | Geography was broad, but research cohorts and language/access contexts were underspecified. | Require recruitment across regions and metro/non-metro origins; distinguish nationwide access from bounded data coverage; language support must be decided from research. |
| UX and usability | Journey is clear, but “useful plan” needed observable task measures. | Added task success, time, errors, drop-off, plan usefulness, mobile testing, and accessible interaction checks. Set WCAG 2.2 AA as the design/verification target. |
| Product outcomes | Metrics existed, but no outcome metric was distinguished from feature/funnel activity. | Added a candidate user outcome and required definitions and thresholds before experiments. Use quantitative data with user feedback, not as a substitute for it. |
| Privacy and trust | Strong intent, but household profiles may include sensitive details and information about children. | Reiterated data minimization, purpose, retention, user control, and deletion. A data map and jurisdiction-specific review are still required before pilot. |
| Engineering and security | Architecture concerns were identified, but no verification baseline was named. | Added OWASP ASVS 5.0.0 as a baseline to tailor and document, plus explicit high-severity release blockers. The implementation still needs a threat model and control verification. |
| Reliability and operations | Backups, monitoring, and support were listed, but reliability gates were not user-centered. | Added user-facing SLOs, error-budget policy, and service/incident expectations. Targets must be set from the service and pilot needs. |
| Launch and business viability | Phases and cost hypothesis were present; broad launch gates could otherwise be judged subjectively. | Require pre-agreed thresholds and cohort reviews, pilot cost/support measurements, and no unresolved severe trust risks. Monetization remains a hypothesis to test. |
| Inventory and price alerts | The initial brief deferred fare tracking and did not cover live multi-provider comparison. | Made flight/stay comparison and user-set price alerts part of the product hypothesis, while adding partner eligibility, permitted use, coverage, freshness, price-quality, and commission disclosure as explicit dependencies. |
| Initial user signal | No traveler workflow evidence was yet recorded. | Added the first self-reported cross-site planning account as N=1 evidence in [discovery notes](DISCOVERY_NOTES.md); it informs questions and hypotheses but does not validate the segment. |
| Booking delay cause | It could have been interpreted as primarily price uncertainty. | The traveler clarified dates were uncertain and work got busy, causing a missed booking. Updated the product hypotheses toward flexible-date watches and optional reminders, while keeping price prediction unvalidated. |
| Price decision | The aggregator could be scoped as a list of live offers alone. | The traveler identified booking timing, affordability, and finding the lowest cost as the hard parts. Added budget-fit and timing-signal discovery; prediction remains unvalidated. |
| Price timing | Target alerts alone might not satisfy the user’s stated need. | They want recent history, target alerts, flexible-date grids, and high/low context for flight/hotel prices, with a notification on every observed drop. Added these as requirements subject to data rights, comparability, and honest historical coverage. |
| Watch cadence | “Every drop” could imply impractical continuous polling. | The user prefers one check per day and an alert for each observed drop. Added daily checks as a preference, subject to provider terms, cost, and observed alert usefulness. |
| Date flexibility | A flexible date range could be treated as a simple +/- day preference. | Clarified that a few days can move only within viable windows; school and important work are hard blocks, and visa resolution may be an external dependency. The product should model these separately. |
| Points and award availability | Loyalty balances could be treated as value without confirming bookable inventory. | Recorded that points are used to save money and awards are checked directly with providers. The product should compare points-plus-fees against cash and label/ link unconfirmed availability rather than inventing award inventory. |
| Video research | “Travel inspiration” could be interpreted as a feed of recommendations. | The traveler described watching many videos to aggregate themes and disagreements, assess food/shopping, routes, visual cues, appeal, creator reactions, and creator similarity based on shared past choices. Added a source-linked synthesis hypothesis; creator opinions remain anecdotal and should not be inferred from facial expressions. |

## Frameworks and primary references

- **Marty Cagan / SVPG — empowered product teams:** teams should receive clear problems and measurable outcomes, then be able to choose solutions. This supports treating this brief’s features as options to test, not a fixed build contract. [Empowered Product Teams](https://www.svpg.com/empowered-product-teams/)
- **Teresa Torres / Product Talk — Continuous Discovery and Opportunity Solution Trees:** connect outcomes to customer opportunities, candidate solutions, and assumption tests; combine continuous interviewing with evaluative testing. [Discovering Solutions](https://www.producttalk.org/discovering-solutions/)
- **GOV.UK Service Manual — user needs and service standards:** research should continue through discovery, alpha/beta, and live; user needs should be evidence-based and framed around problems. The service standards also call out accessibility, privacy, success measures, and reliability. [Learn about user needs](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/start-by-learning-user-needs), [understand user needs](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard/point-1-understand-user-needs)
- **Nielsen Norman Group — usability metrics:** task success, time, and errors are useful performance measures; these should support, not replace, qualitative observation. [Usability Metrics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-metrics/)
- **W3C — WCAG 2.2:** current W3C guidance recommends using WCAG 2.2 for web accessibility work. It covers mobile, low-vision, and cognitive needs; conformance still needs practical testing with users and assistive technologies. [WCAG 2.2 Recommendation](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG/)
- **NIST — data minimization:** collect/use/retain personal information only as relevant and necessary for a purpose, and only as long as needed. This is especially important for household and child-related profile data. [NIST minimization glossary](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/minimization)
- **OWASP — Application Security Verification Standard:** a structured standard for web application security design and verification. Use the current ASVS version and choose a proportionate level rather than claiming broad security from a checklist alone. [OWASP ASVS 5.0.0](https://asvs.owasp.org/)
- **Google SRE — SLOs and error budgets:** define reliability from the user’s perspective and use error budgets to make release-risk decisions based on observed service performance. [Production Services Best Practices](https://sre.google/sre-book/service-best-practices/)
- **Skyscanner partner/API documentation:** its flight live-prices API provides bookable offers through create/poll search and deeplinks, while API access is subject to partner approval and commercial eligibility; its affiliate program offers a different, more constrained path with widgets, banners, and text links. Prices can differ by the time a user reaches the booking partner. [Live Prices API](https://developers.skyscanner.net/docs/flights-live-prices/overview), [Travel API eligibility](https://www.partners.skyscanner.net/contact/travel-api), [Affiliate program](https://www.partners.skyscanner.net/product/affiliates), [price accuracy FAQ](https://developers.skyscanner.net/docs/faqs)
- **Agoda partner documentation:** affiliate partners can access rate/availability data under partner terms; its agreement explicitly governs price-comparison use. Partner access and allowed display/data usage need to be confirmed for the product’s intended use. [Agoda Affiliate API portal](https://partners.agoda.com/DeveloperPortal/APIDoc/), [Affiliate Agreement](https://partners.agoda.com/Content/pdf/Agoda_OnlineAffiliateAgreement.pdf)
- **Booking.com and Expedia Group partner APIs:** live availability/prices are offered through partner programs with credential, affiliate, and price-display requirements. These are candidate alternatives; access and launch requirements must be validated directly. [Booking.com Demand API prerequisites](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/getting-started/prerequisites), [Expedia Rapid Shopping API](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/lodging/shopping/about-shopping-api)

## Important limitations

- This was a document review, not user research, a market-size study, a legal opinion, a security audit, an accessibility audit, or an engineering design review.
- A small interview round can uncover needs and shape hypotheses; it cannot establish India-wide prevalence or represent all Indian travelers.
- “Price aggregator” needs a precise product boundary. A live search comparison, indicative price calendar, and recurring watch service have different supplier permissions, refresh rules, costs, and accuracy expectations.
- A price alert is only useful if the watched option is comparable and the alert is timely. The first release should notify against a traveler-set target, disclose the time/coverage of the last check, and avoid claiming it has identified the market’s future lowest price.
- Affiliate access is not equivalent to independent whole-market coverage. Provider selection, commercial relationships, and unavailable suppliers can affect results; the UI and ranking policy need to make this understandable.
- India-wide product availability does not mean every location, language, transport mode, or provider is covered equally. The product must state coverage honestly and test for uneven outcomes.
- Frameworks are aids to judgment. The team must select controls and measures that fit the product, data, users, and applicable law.
